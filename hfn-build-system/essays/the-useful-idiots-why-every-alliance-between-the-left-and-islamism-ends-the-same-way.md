# The Useful Idiots: Why Every Alliance Between the Left and Islamism Ends the Same Way

In February 2026, the British political left celebrated what they perceived as a historic triumph. In the Greater Manchester constituency of Gorton and Denton, Green Party candidate Hannah Spencer overturned a Labour majority spanning nearly a century to secure a stunning by-election victory. Green Party leader Zack Polanski and left-wing commentators hailed the win as proof that a "multicultural coalition" could defeat the establishment.

To achieve this upset, the eco-socialist Green Party aggressively courted the constituency's Muslim population — which makes up around 30% of the electorate — by capitalising on anger over the Gaza conflict, distributing campaign videos in Urdu, and portraying themselves as the ultimate anti-imperialist champions. They promised progressive revolution. Their new voters want a rather different kind of revolution.

The Green Party is staunchly progressive, officially stating they are "unequivocally pro-trans" and deeply committed to LGBTQ+ rights and intersectional feminism. Yet they have actively aligned themselves with a voting bloc that polling consistently shows holds deeply conservative social values — with large majorities opposing homosexuality, same-sex marriage, and progressive gender ideology. The left believes they are leading a progressive revolution. In reality, they are playing a role that history has a name for: the useful idiot.

The term — used during the Cold War to describe those who propagandise for a cause without comprehending its ultimate objectives — has no confirmed attribution to Lenin, despite popular belief. But the concept is precise: highly intelligent, politically aware individuals who lend their organisational apparatus, intellectual legitimacy, and street-level agitation to a movement that will, once the dust settles, turn on them. Perhaps nowhere in modern history is this pattern more devastatingly illustrated than in the recurrent alliances between secular leftist movements and Islamist factions. The historical record is unambiguous. And it is not comfortable reading for anyone on the British or European left who imagines that today's tactical coalition will end differently.

## Part 1: The Oldest Alliance

The nexus between leftism and political Islam has its roots in the pragmatic strategies of the nascent Soviet Union. During the Russian Civil War, the Bolsheviks inherited a significant Muslim minority in the Caucasus and Central Asia. To recruit them against the Western-backed Whites, Soviet leaders concluded deals with armed Islamist groups throughout the region. The Red Army utilised them to fight anti-Bolshevik forces, freeing regular units for other fronts.

This tactical synthesis culminated in the 1920 Congress of the Peoples of the East, held by the Communist International in Baku. The conference was headlined by Grigory Zinoviev, who explicitly called for "a true people's holy war against the robbers and oppressors… a holy war, in the first place against British imperialism!" The Islamist attendees met this exhortation with enthusiastic applause. Lenin continued the effort to align Muslim communities with communist goals by co-opting Islamic clerics to act as proponents of Soviet power. Stalin later reversed this — but the template was established. Anti-Western imperialism could serve as the unifying solvent, capable of temporarily dissolving the ideological barriers between historical materialism and Islamic fundamentalism.

The intellectual vulnerability of the left in these alliances stems from a failure to recognise that Islamism functions not merely as a religion, but as a competing comprehensive political system. In 1920, Bertrand Russell was among the first to spot this. In *The Practice and Theory of Bolshevism*, he argued that Communism functions as a "secular religion" and a "universal State" highly comparable to Islam. Both movements view their frontiers as strictly provisional. Both make no functional distinction between religion and politics. Both emphasise group goals over individual interests, claim absolute universalism, and seek to convert unbelievers globally. Both provide a vision of a perfectly just society on earth — the classless Communist utopia versus the restored Caliphate.

Crucially, both share a deep antipathy toward Western civilisation and individualism. There they are, united in trying to dismantle it. The problem is that what they want at the end of that process is radically different — and completely incompatible. The left envisions a secular, egalitarian, materialist order; Islamism envisions a theocratic one governed by divine law. Once they have destroyed the West, the only remaining solution is to destroy each other. The alliance is not merely fragile. It is structurally suicidal.

Islamist intellectuals cannibalised Marxist methodologies for their own ends. Sayyid Qutb, the foundational ideologue of the Egyptian Muslim Brotherhood, openly integrated Leninist concepts into his writings. In *Milestones* (*Ma'alim fi al-Tariq*), Qutb advocated for an "Islamic vanguard" to combat *jahiliyyah* — the state of ignorance — a concept that directly mirrors Lenin's theory of the vanguard party. By adopting the revolutionary mechanics of the left, Islamists presented themselves as fellow anti-establishment revolutionaries, effectively masking their fundamentally reactionary objectives until it was too late.

## Part 2: Iran — The Textbook Case

In November 1979, Noureddin Kianuri — the sixty-four-year-old General Secretary of the Tudeh Party, who had spent decades in exile in East Germany living under the pseudonym "Dr. Silvio Macetti" — sat for an interview with the Greek newspaper *Elevtherotipia* and boasted of his party's "favourable reception" in revolutionary Tehran. He dismissed the Islamists as a temporary, "petty-bourgeois" phenomenon. They would fade. The left would inherit the state.

The seeds of this confidence were sown in opposition to Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi. The 1953 CIA- and MI6-backed coup that overthrew Prime Minister Mossadegh embedded deep anti-imperialist sentiment in Iranian political culture. The Shah's subsequent "White Revolution" alienated both the traditional clergy and the urban working class, pushing the radical Islamist camp — led by the exiled Ayatollah Khomeini — into tactical alignment with the radical left. The intellectual bridge was built by Ali Shariati, the father of "Red Shiism," who blended Islamic theology with Marxist concepts, framing the Prophet Mohammad's mission as a class struggle. When demonstrations reached critical mass in 1977, communists, socialists, and Islamists marched side by side.

Kianuri's Tudeh Party possessed immense organisational capacity among the working class. Shortly after the Shah fell, Khomeini sent his son Ahmad to meet with Massoud Rajavi, leader of the Mujahedeen-e-Khalq. The offer was blunt: "If you support the Imam and oppose his opponents, all gates will be open before you." Rajavi rejected the demand for absolute subjugation. That put the left on a collision course with Khomeini.

What followed was systematic eradication. Hezbollah gangs — informal, club-wielding militias — targeted MEK and Tudeh gatherings. By 1981, the newly formed Pasdaran (Revolutionary Guards) unleashed the full force of the state. The very leftists who had suffered torture in the Shah's prisons found themselves incarcerated by the new Islamic Republic. The purge reached its zenith during the 1988 executions, when "hanging judges" inside Evin Prison presided over drumhead tribunals. Thousands of left-wing political prisoners were executed. The Abdorrahman Boroumand Center documents state violence that claimed tens of thousands of lives.

In May 1983, Iranian state television broadcast the seventy-one-year-old Kianuri — broken, tortured, barely recognisable — opening his forced confession with the words "Imam Khomeini, the Great Leader of the Revolution." He denounced his own life's work as "high treason" and his party's ideology as a "foreign illness." His chief intellectual, Ehsan Tabari — the philosopher who had spent a lifetime arguing that scientific materialism would triumph over religion — was paraded on the same television as having "converted to Islam" after months of solitary confinement. The Tudeh Party was destroyed. Kianuri died in Tehran in 1999, a broken man in a country he had helped deliver to the clerics.

## Part 3: Indonesia — The Killing Fields

By 1965, Dipa Nusantara Aidit had built the Indonesian Communist Party into the largest in the non-communist world. He was forty-two years old, a cabinet-level figure in Sukarno's government, and supremely confident. Over three million members. Penetration of every labour union, peasant organisation, and intellectual circle in the archipelago. The state itself had given the PKI a seat at the table.

That seat was NASAKOM — President Sukarno's formula of *Nasionalisme*, *Agama* (Religion), and *Komunisme*, which held that nationalism, Islam, and Marxism were wholly compatible. Aidit embraced it as a strategic triumph: a state-sanctioned, multi-class united front behind which the PKI could gradually transition the country toward socialism. The mandatory inclusion of *Agama* forced the PKI into an uneasy alliance with powerful Islamic organisations, most notably Nahdlatul Ulama (NU) and its militant youth wing, Ansor. Aidit severely underestimated the deep-seated theological animosity these groups harboured toward the atheist foundations of Marxism. He believed Sukarno's patronage and shared anti-imperialist rhetoric would override religious friction. It was a fatal miscalculation.

On 1 October 1965, a group of left-wing military officers — the "30 September Movement" — kidnapped and murdered six senior army generals in what was widely perceived as an abortive coup. The Indonesian Army, led by Major General Suharto, rapidly seized control, laying the blame at the feet of the PKI. What followed was a swift, army-orchestrated campaign of mass killing. Crucially, the military did not act alone. They actively enlisted and armed Islamic organisations to carry out the slaughter at the grassroots level. Ansor acted as the primary death squads in East Java. US Consulate telegrams from Surabaya noted: "We continue to receive reports [of] PKI being slaughtered by Ansor [a Muslim militia] in many areas East Java." Conservative estimates place the number of murdered communists and leftists between 500,000 and 1.5 million, with an additional one million imprisoned.

Aidit fled to Central Java when the army moved. He was captured within weeks, interrogated by military officers, and summarily executed on 22 November 1965. He was forty-two. His deputy, Njoto — a refined intellectual who played the violin and wrote poetry — was shot three weeks later, aged thirty-eight. The third-largest communist party in the world had been physically liquidated. Its leader did not even receive a trial.

## Part 4: The Pattern Repeats

Abdel Khaliq Mahjub was the most formidable communist intellectual in the Arab world. He had led the Sudanese Communist Party since 1949, championed women's rights and secular education, and provided the organisational backbone for the 1964 October Revolution that toppled the Abboud military dictatorship — fighting alongside the Muslim Brotherhood in a "modern forces" coalition. He was forty when they triumphed. The coalition had served its purpose. It would not survive the peace.

Once the military fell, the Brotherhood pivoted to align with traditional religious parties. They weaponised a single student's alleged blasphemy to orchestrate a manufactured moral panic, providing the political cover to outlaw the SCP in 1965. Mahjub's party — the very organisation that had supplied the intellectual and organisational energy for the revolution — was banned by the forces it had helped to power. When Jaafar Nimeiri seized control in 1969 and initially courted the left, Mahjub supported a counter-coup in 1971 that briefly toppled Nimeiri. It failed within three days.

On 28 July 1971, Mahjub was hanged at Kobar Prison in Khartoum. He was forty-four. His comrade Joseph Garang — the first South Sudanese man to earn a law degree, who had served as Minister of Southern Affairs — was executed beside him. The party that had provided the intellectual and organisational backbone for Sudan's democratic revolution was destroyed by the very forces it had helped to power.

In the West, the pattern persists in miniature. In France, Jean-Luc Mélenchon's La France Insoumise has courted the Arab-Muslim electorate through pro-Palestinian stances that align closely with Islamist grievances — prompting state inquiries into *islamo-gauchisme*. In Hamtramck, Michigan, an all-Muslim city council — elected with the help of progressive voters — immediately moved to ban LGBTQ+ Pride flags on city property. The progressives had provided the electoral vehicle. Once in power, the new majority enforced their own values.

## Part 5: What Comes Next

The demographic trajectory in European cities — Birmingham, Bradford, Marseille, Brussels, Amsterdam — suggests Muslim populations will continue to grow. The left's fatal assumption is that cultural conservatism is a "phase" that education and exposure will cure. History suggests otherwise. When ideological enemies unite solely out of a shared desire to tear down the existing order, betrayal is not a possibility. It is an absolute certainty.

Kianuri confessed on television. Aidit was shot without a trial. Mahjub was hanged. They were not fools. They were among the most capable political operators of their generation. They simply could not conceive that their allies hated them more than their shared enemy.

Every alliance between the secular left and Islamism has ended the same way: the left builds the revolution, provides the organisational apparatus and intellectual legitimacy, and then gets stood up against the wall. Iran, Indonesia, Sudan — the pattern has no exceptions. The only question is whether the British and European left will learn from history, or whether they will become its next chapter.
